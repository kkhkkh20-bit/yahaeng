## Surface-specific adaptations

The user's Among Us, Wild Rift, and ninja-action references establish the large primary radial control, surrounding auxiliary circles, readable silhouettes, and bottom instant-use inventory. They are interaction and composition references; no reference-game graphics were imported. The ink, ivory, and cool steel treatment continues the established game world. The main menu's square control rule remains scoped to that menu.

The former uniform line-icon description is superseded. Action, crouch, grab, dress, whistle, and equipment use the existing game-icons.net silhouettes with their existing [credits](../../src/art/hud/ATTRIBUTION.json). The primary fist is authored solid geometry; ranged/device symbols retain the earlier field stroke icons. Four authored [40×40 item pictograms](../../src/art/hud/items/README.md) identify the empty can, cigarette packet, smoke canister, and radio through shape and restrained color. Their colors identify inventory; they do not expand the root palette or imply a new health-potion mechanic.

The HUD's compact type sizes, bold control labels, status colors, circular rims, and small inventory shapes are surface-specific implementation facts. They are not additions to the front-end type or rounded scales. Small type and inherited template warnings are not canonized as rules for future surfaces.

Expanded objective reading keeps its pause/continue instruction above scrolling content. Equipment keeps its title, close control, pause indication, and movement footer outside its scrolling body. Overflow feedback is measured from the body. Contextual labels remain short, accessible action captions remain separate, and duplicate dress/target information is avoided.

The final collision fixes place the target-cycle control in the right column, update camera clearance for visible contextual controls, and reserve bottom item slots, movement hints, and save confirmation during world-label placement. A visible NPC speech candidate temporarily hides the transient save confirmation and invalidates cached label bounds. These source changes address the two reviewed collisions; they do not establish a universal no-collision guarantee.
